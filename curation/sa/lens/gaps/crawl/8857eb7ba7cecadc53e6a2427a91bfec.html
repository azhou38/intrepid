Fleurieu Peninsula Itinerary

21 AUG 2026

Follow the curve of the Fleurieu Peninsula from McLaren Vale to Deep Creek and discover the simple pleasures that make this corner of South Australia so easy to love.

Just 45 minutes from Adelaide, the Fleurieu Peninsula has a way of helping you slow down. It’s a place where the best memories often arrive unannounced. One moment you're leaving the city behind. The next, you're winding past vineyards that roll towards the sea, pulling over for a bakery stop in a country town or watching the afternoon sun dance across the water. Here, life flows a little differently. Lunches linger longer and beach walks become the highlight of the day. This five-day coastal itinerary invites you even further south, weaving together cellar doors, seaside towns and wild national parks. It's a journey best taken with the windows down, an esky in the boot and nowhere pressing to be.

Adelaide to McLaren Vale

The city slips away quickly, replaced by vine-covered hills, gum-lined roads and the promise of a generous pour waiting just around the bend.

What to see and do

McLaren Vale is the kind of place where a wine tasting can easily mean a whole afternoon.
Step inside the striking d'Arenberg Cube, where surreal art and sweeping vineyard views collide, or take a seat at Down The Rabbit Hole, where the deck chairs face the vines and nobody's in a hurry. Prefer to take things a little slower? Follow the Shiraz Trail by bike, winding between cellar doors, paddocks and native bushland. The joy of McLaren Vale isn't ticking wineries off a list. It's discovering a favourite drop, meeting the people who made it and staying for one more glass because the conversation is too good to leave.

Where to eat and drink

Settle in for a long lunch at Maxwell's Restaurant or Salopian Inn, where local produce takes centre stage.
As the afternoon unfolds, continue exploring at Coriole, Chalk Hill Wines or Samuel's Gorge, where vineyard views stretch towards the coast. Come evening, gather around a table at Pizzatecca and share wood-fired pizzas as the last light settles across the vines. For more tips, check out our foodie guide to McLaren Vale here. 

Where to stay

Wake among the trees at CABN Bella, settle into vineyard surrounds at The Old Chaff Mill Organic Vineyard Retreat, or check in to Serafino McLaren Vale Hotel, McLaren Vale Motel and Apartments or Rare Earth Retreats.

McLaren Vale to Victor Harbor

Swap rows of vines for rolling ocean views as the coastline begins to steal the show.

What to see and do

Start the day wandering the streets of Port Elliot, where bakeries fill the air with the scent of fresh pastries and locals drift between cafés and boutiques, a takeaway cuppa in hand.
Further along the coast at Victor Harbor, walk to the top of The Bluff for panoramic views. From here, you can watch as the wind picks up the sea like a blanket, shaking it out and laying it down toward Encounter Bay.  Stroll across the Granite Island Causeway, keeping an eye out for seabirds catching a ride on the breeze, or settle into a window seat aboard the historic Cockle Train as it hugs the coastline between townships.
For a different perspective, join The Big Duck Boat Tours and watch the rugged shoreline reveal itself from the water. You may even spy a few marine friends. 

Where to eat and drink

Fresh seafood, generous portions and ocean views are never far away. Spend the evening at Lost Phoenix Farm, The Larder Kitchen, Nino's of Victor Harbor, Home of Plenty or Loco Mexican, where good food comes with a healthy dose of coastal charm.

Where to stay

Choose a beachfront stay at NRMA Victor Harbor Beachfront Holiday Park, Breeze Beachfront Apartment or Azure by the Sea, or settle into nearby Port Elliot or Middleton at YHA Port Elliot or Beach Huts Middleton.

Victor Harbor to Deep Creek National Park

Today's itinerary trades bustling seaside towns for steep cliffs, empty beaches and some of South Australia's most spectacularly raw places.

What to see and do

Follow sections of the Deep Creek Circuit, where towering gums give way to ocean lookouts and kangaroos graze just metres from the trail. Keep watch for echidnas shuffling through the scrub and seabirds gliding along the cliffs.
For those willing to stretch their legs, Blowhole Beach delivers one of the Fleurieu's most rewarding walks. At the bottom, waves crash against dark rock formations while the Southern Ocean rolls endlessly towards Antarctica.

Where to eat and drink

Services within the park are limited, so stock up before arriving. Nearby Normanville is your best stop for supplies, takeaway treats or a relaxed meal after a day on the trails.

Where to stay

Stay close to nature at Naiko Retreat or Grass Tree Gully, where you'll wake to birdsong and uninterrupted views across the landscape.

Deep Creek National Park to Port Willunga

The journey back north reveals hidden coves, dramatic headlands and some of the Fleurieu's most unforgettable stretches of coastline.

Travel Tip: There is the option here to extend your trip and add on a visit to nearby Kangaroo Island – the main ferry departs from Cape Jervis, just a hop skip and a hill away from the Deep Creek / Normanville area. For more information, check out our suggested Kangaroo Island Itinerary. 

What to see and do

Leave Deep Creek behind and follow the coast through some of South Australia's calmest coves. Pause at Second Valley, where boats bob gently in the clear seas, before continuing on to Rapid Bay, where the jetty stretches out turquoise water and locals swap fishing stories along the shoreline. Further north, pass through cute country towns like Myponga and coastal villages like Sellicks Beach and Port Willunga, where rolling hills meet the sea and every bend in the road reveals another postcard-worthy view. For a different perspective, take to the skies with Helivista. From above, you'll see vineyards, beaches and rugged coastline stitched together in one extraordinary landscape.

Where to eat and drink

Book a table at the iconic Star of Greece, perched above the cliffs at Port Willunga, where long lunches often stretch into sunset. Alternatively, head to the Victory Hotel for a classic pub meal with sweeping coastal views, or keep things relaxed at Little Rickshaw Aldinga.

Where to stay

Spend the night at Aldinga Beach Holiday Park, Mesa Del Mar, Perla Del Mare, Cerulean or Drift Port Willunga, all perfectly placed for sunset walks and sea views.

Port Willunga to Adelaide

A gentle farewell to the coast.

What to see and do

Start your final morning the Fleurieu way. Without the rush. 
Take a swim before breakfast. Wander barefoot along the sand. Watch the tide roll in as the first coffee of the day warms your hands.

Where to eat and drink

heading back to Adelaide, settle in for lunch at Pearl Restaurant and savour one last taste of the coast. Then make the easy drive back to the city, carrying home a collection of moments that somehow feel much bigger than the kilometres travelled.

FAQs

When is the best time to visit the Fleurieu Peninsula?

Spring and autumn are ideal for mild temperatures and quieter roads. Summer is perfect for beach days, clear water and swimming, while winter brings dramatic seas, cosy pubs and fewer crowds.

How do I get around the Fleurieu Peninsula?

A self drive itinerary is essential, allowing you to explore beaches, wineries and national parks at your own pace.
