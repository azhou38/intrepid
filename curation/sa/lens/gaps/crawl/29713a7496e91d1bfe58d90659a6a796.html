LVN Restaurant at Bird In Hand wins One MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

What do you do with a vineyard that looks like a painting? At Bird in Hand, you build a restaurant inside it.

Then, you adorn the frame with One MICHELIN Star. LVN Restaurant at Bird In Hand is South Australia’s newest star-worthy dining destination, resting at the end of a winding private drive in the Adelaide Hills. Past bronze garden sculptures and two decades of planted botanicals, the former dairy farm sits on 100 acres and looks straight out across the undulating vines of Peramangk land.  

Cellar door visitors have known Bird in Hand for more than two decades. In late 2023, that grew into a 40-seat glasshouse dining room. It’s named after Lalla Victoria Nugent, daughter of founders Andrew and Susie Nugent, whose initials also crown the winery's upcoming pinnacle cuvée.

Inside, the boundary between gallery and dining room disappears completely. Contemporary Australian art lines the walls throughout, driven by the same creative instinct that shows up on the plate. With no wall separating the tables from the pass, Executive Chef Jacob Davey's kitchen operates like an open gallery. Hyper-seasonal ingredients, like wild botanicals plucked from the estate grounds, rare native herbs and local line-caught coastal seafood, are treated like raw pigments across a 7-to-10 course progression. It’s a three-hour study in restraint, proving that at LVN, the land itself is the primary artist.

Visit LVN Restaurant

A menu painted by place

You can walk the very same gardens chef Jacob wanders before service, a living pantry of river mint, wild elderflower and green strawberries. These grounds anchor a menu drawn from Bird in Hand's three homes: Woodside (Peramangk country), Finniss (Ngarrindjeri country) and Tasmania (Paredareme country). He also works with local fishers like Jack Henderson out of Port Lincoln to source line-caught fish that might turn up cured, or dressed in herbs clipped metres from your table.

Then comes the twist: the kitchen constructs its plates backwards. Rather than finding a bottle for a finished dish, Jacob builds the menu directly around the estate's cellar, working closely with head winemaker Sarah Burvill. Paired with the estate's benchmark chardonnays, structural red blends and cool-climate shiraz, every element on the plate is built to bring out the acidity, tannins and oak of a vintage that's been waiting years in the dark to be poured.

The [MICHELIN] Guide really focuses on consistency. It means that you’re consistently doing the job at a level that’s recognised all over the world. - Jacob Davey

The Chef

Jacob’s love of cooking started with family meals, preparing lamb chops and ‘spag bol’ for his parents and four siblings as a teenager. He's come a long way since, moving through kitchens in London, Melbourne and Sydney, including a stretch at MICHELIN-Starred Pied à Terre, before taking the reins at Sydney's est. from the legendary Peter Doyle himself.

A local at heart, Jacob's back in Adelaide now with his wife and two young kids, discovering a pace of life he didn't know he'd missed until returning home. Restaurant Botanic gave him the chance to properly reacquaint himself with South Australian produce, and by the time he landed at LVN as Executive Chef, his respect for what's local had become closer to a rule of life. Wallaby and deer, both considered pest species here, get the same care on his pass as anything foraged from the garden. "It keeps the menu quite dynamic, because we’re reactionary to what’s growing in the garden and the little micro seasons that are happening," Jacob says. Whatever doesn't get used fresh gets fermented, pickled or dehydrated instead of binned. The first time he saw Bird in Hand's gardens, he knew this was somewhere he could finally build a restaurant entirely on his own terms.

A day in the Adelaide Hills

Where to stay

'O' Quarters, Bird in Hand's own residence next door to LVN, named after the Nugent’s son Oscar. Art-filled and set right among the estate's gardens, you're already exactly where you need to be for dinner.

Detour through Hahndorf

Around 30 minutes from the city, stop in Hahndorf on your way to the winery. Australia's oldest surviving German settlement, wander Hahndorf’s main street before heading to Beerenberg Farm to pick your own strawberries (from November to April). For lunch, stay in Hahndorf, or settle in at The Lane Vineyard Restaurant with Chef Hat views over the vines. Close by is The Cedars, once home to landscape painter Sir Hans Heysen, his gardens and studio are now home to the collection of works that made him so famous.

Spend the rest of the afternoon in Bird in Hand's own gardens and admire the 20 years of planting, where Jacob forages from daily, before LVN takes over for the night. 

Your One-MICHELIN-Star lunch

Top dish

Expect native ingredients from the estate's three homes, wine paired to match and two menu staples worth watching for: the house-made croissant, served alongside a seafood course with garden-fresh herb butter, and the grilled oyster, dressed with native garnishes.

Cellar door hopping and farm tour 

Stay close to your home away from home, with Artwine, Petaluma and Simon Tolley all just minutes from the Bird in Hand property. If you'd rather slow things down, swap a cellar door for the Jurlique Farm Tour, 90 minutes behind the scenes of the Adelaide Hills' own biodynamic skincare farm.

Head 10 minutes down the road to That's Enrico, a pizzeria built around slow-fermented dough and organic, local wine - a fitting, low-key finish to a weekend built on good produce.
