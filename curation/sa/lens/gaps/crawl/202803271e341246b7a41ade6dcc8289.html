Arkhe wins One MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

Everything on this menu has one thing in common: it's been through fire first.

Fire. Water. Air. The beginning of everything. The ancient Greeks had a word for it: Arkhé. It’s a fitting moniker for Executive Chef Jake Kellie’s Adelaide restaurant, which now boasts one of Australia's first MICHELIN Stars.

Five years ago, when the city’s first open-flame restaurant opened, the name stumped most guests. Booking calls came with a quick lesson in how to say it, while local write-ups would take the time to spell it out phonetically (Ar-kay). Now, the name gets passed between chefs from Sydney to Singapore like a password. Everyone in Australian hospitality knows it, and exactly what it means to get a table.

Arkhé sits inside a heritage-listed sandstone building in Norwood. It's the kind of suburban address that gives away nothing from the street, but inside, the dining room is built like a theatre. Counter seats ring the open kitchen, while the rest of the room angles toward it like an audience toward a stage. A three-and-a-half-tonne wood oven is the star of the show, and from most tables, you can watch the flames doing the work long before your plate arrives. 

Visit Arkhé

Playing with fire

On your way to your table, you’ll pass the dry-ageing cabinet hung with rows of Mayura Station wagyu — some of Australia’s most awarded beef, raised on the Limestone Coast and chocolate-fed for its final months. After 30 days of ageing, it’s sliced to order before meeting the flame. There are no automatic timers or temp controls here. The ingredients move through the oven by eye, feel and instinct. It’s a strikingly analogue approach to fire that saw Arkhé ranked 16th in the world (and 5th in Australia) on the World’s 101 Best Steak list. "The vibe at Arkhé is electric," Jake says. "We grill s***, and we make it tasty."

The fire isn't reserved for beef alone. Rock lobster is pulled live from Port Lincoln waters, grilled slowly over the flame and finished with garlic brown butter, while Eyre Peninsula abalone gets braised and laid over house-made wontons. They’re both proof that the same fire built for a Mayura tomahawk works just as well on whatever the coast sends in. "We are the food capital of Australia," Jake says - and on this seasonal menu, it's hard to argue with that.

I give everything to being a chef...and that's cooking good food for beautiful people. - Jake Kellie

The chef

Before Adelaide, Jake cooked his way through some of the world's most decorated kitchens: Gordon Ramsay's Maze, The Fat Duck and The Ledbury. Then came Burnt Ends in Singapore, where he ran the pass at a MICHELIN-Starred, World's 50 Best kitchen.

Jake could have opened Arkhé anywhere. "I think the best thing that I love about South Australia, being a chef, it's your pantry," he says. It's the kind of decision that only makes sense once you've tasted what he's talking about. Our natural pantry is the reason a chef with his accolades chose a heritage building in Norwood, over anywhere else in the world.

From Jake's kitchen to yours: Spencer Gulf prawns

Pulled straight from Jake's own recipe book, this is the prawn dish he reaches for at Christmas time for a table full of family and friends. It's best when made from local produce, cooked over the fire with garlic brown butter, capers and parsley. "It's super simple, but bloody delicious," Jake says. 

Ingredients

Serves 4 as a sharing dish. Prep: 15 minutes. Garlic butter infusion: 1 hour. Cooking: 10 minutes.

For the prawns  

● 12 large Spencer Gulf prawns, whole and shell-on  
● 2 tbsp olive oil  
● Sea salt  
● 1 lemon, cut into wedges  

For the garlic brown butter  

● 200g unsalted butter  
● 60g garlic, finely minced  

To finish  

● 100ml garlic brown butter  
● 20g salted capers, gently rinsed, finely chopped  
● 10ml fresh lemon juice  
● 5g flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped  
● Extra lemon wedges, to serve  

Method

Prepare the garlic brown butter

Place the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Cook, stirring frequently, until the milk solids turn golden brown and the butter develops a deep, nutty aroma. If using a thermometer, aim for approximately 160°C. 

Remove from the heat and strain through a fine sieve lined with muslin. 

While the brown butter is still hot, add the minced garlic and allow it to infuse for 1 hour. Strain again to remove the garlic.  

Prepare the prawns

Using kitchen scissors, cut along the back of each prawn and remove the intestinal tract, keeping the head and shell intact. Lightly coat the prawns in olive oil and season with sea salt.

Cook over fire

Prepare a charcoal or wood-fired barbecue with a good bed of hot coals. Place the prawns over the fire and cook for approximately 2–3 minutes on each side, depending on their size, until the shells are lightly charred and the flesh is just cooked through.  

Alternatively, cook on a conventional barbecue over high heat.

Finish the sauce

Gently warm 100ml of the garlic brown butter in a small saucepan.  Add the rinsed capers, followed by the fresh lemon juice.  

Remove from the heat and stir through the chopped parsley.

Serve

Arrange the prawns on a large serving platter. Spoon the warm garlic brown butter generously over the prawns, ensuring the capers and parsley are evenly distributed.  

Serve immediately with fresh lemon wedges. 

Arkhe itinerary

Where to stay

Hotel Indigo, right across from Adelaide Central Market. Roll out of bed and into one of the world's best food markets, where stallholders have been trading since 1869.

Take the long way to dinner

Join the Eastside Wine and Ale Trail, which is a self-guided route through the same inner-east pocket you'll end up in for dinner. Find small cellar doors tucked behind warehouse doors and breweries running out of old garages. Follow the map at your own pace before the short trip to Arkhé (it may be an Uber away depending where you finish up).

Your One-MICHELIN-Star dinner

Top dish

Arkhé is the night's headline act. Order the Mayura Station tomahawk, dry-aged thirty days, crusted in smoked chocolate and finished over open flame. It's the dish people fly in for.

Cook like a local:

Start at Adelaide Central Market, recently named one of the world's best food markets, home to over 70 traders and the produce that ends up on every good menu in the city. A private guide walks you through the stalls before a chef transforms the morning's finds into lunch back at Market & Meander, sparkling wine included, dessert tasting and all.

Wander the East End: 

North Terrace does double duty as gallery row, home to the Art Gallery of South Australia, the South Australian Museum and MOD. Pick whichever pulls you in, or duck through all three.

Whiskey before dinner:

Book the whiskey tasting experience at NOLA - a proper detour before the night gets going. Then, just down the road still in Adelaide's East End, Africola also brings a wood-fired philosophy to the table. It’s bold North African-inspired, and cooked the same way: over flame. If Arkhé's on your list, this one belongs there too.
