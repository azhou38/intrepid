2 day Adelaide Itinerary

07 AUG 2026

  • 11 suggestions
  • 2 minute read

Adelaide runs on its people.

You'll meet them at the Adelaide Central Market, where a trader talks you through the difference between two kinds of olive oil you didn't know existed. You'll meet them in a laneway bar, where the bartender ends up recommending your next three stops. You'll meet them in our best restaurants, where the chef steps out to check how your table's going. Adelaide is filled with locals who will share the best of it with you. 

Beyond the city, the Barossa and McLaren Vale are both close enough for a half-day trip, the beach is a short tram ride away and there's almost always a festival or event on somewhere in the city. Whether Adelaide's a stop on your longer South Australian road trip or the destination itself, two days is enough to get a proper feel for it. Here's how to spend them.

Adelaide - markets, events and nature

 

 

What to do

Start at the Adelaide Central Market, where the traders have just put out their produce fresh that morning and after lunch, let the season decide the rest. Warm weather means the tram to Glenelg or beachside for a swim, or the RoofClimb at Adelaide Oval if you want the city laid out beneath you. Cooler months are great for the Adelaide Zoo, or up in the Hills for a half-day in Hahndorf. Check out our What's On guide before you lock anything in — there's rarely a week without a festival, exhibition or event somewhere in the mix.

Local tip: The Adelaide Central Market is closed on Sundays and Mondays, so start the day with breakfast from some of our best brunch locations instead. 

Where to eat

Book ahead for modern Italian at Osteria Oggi, contemporary Asian at Golden Boy or something more European at Garçon Bleu. At Fugazzi, order the gnocchi fritto and tear it apart by hand before dipping it into their famed whipped ricotta and honey, or try OZIN for its charcoal based cooking. For something worth the drive, Penfolds Magill Estate Restaurant seats just 34 people, all looking out over the vines, with a sommelier pouring Penfolds' own Bin 707 by the glass.

Where to drink

East End Cellars is the easy start to the evening. Hains & Co if you're after cocktails, NOLA if craft beer's more your speed. Latteria Bar is the one to know about if you want to keep the night going. 

Where to stay

Pick your view: Eos by SkyCity puts you riverside, the Sofitel sits you a few steps from shopping and dining and the Oval Hotel looks straight across the parklands to the scoreboard. If you want to be in the middle of the laneways and North Terrace, book the Adelaide Marriott instead.

Adelaide to Port Adelaide

 

 

What to do

Start the day with a good coffee; Adelaide's never more than a few hundred metres from a decent one. Then wander through the Adelaide Botanic Garden, past the glasshouse and its rainforest sitting in the middle of the city, on your way to North Terrace. MOD., the South Australian Museum and the Art Gallery of South Australia all sit close enough together to cover in one stretch. Then save the afternoon for Port Adelaide, where old maritime warehouses line the streets, murals colour hidden laneways and the South Australian Maritime Museum invites you aboard with guided tours that bring the port’s seafaring stories to life. Round out the day at Pirate Life Brewing, where bold beers flow beneath the steel beams of a former warehouse in the heart of the port.

Where to eat

For dinner, book Africola if you want colour and spice in equal measure, or Arkhe for beef dry-aged in-house and cooked over a three-and-a-half-tonne wood oven. Station Road brings the trolley service. There’s Champagne poured tableside and a cheese cart that rolls past later in the night. Or go classic French at La Louisiane or keep it casual in Port Adelaide with authentic Mexican at La Popular Taqueria.

FAQs

When is the best time to visit Adelaide?

Adelaide shines year round. Summer brings beach days and festivals, autumn delivers mild weather and wine harvests, winter offers cosy dining and cultural events and spring fills the city with colour and outdoor energy.

How do I get around?

Adelaide is easy to navigate on foot, by tram and by bus. For regional travel, hiring a car offers the most flexibility.

Staying longer? Discover even more things to do in Adelaide

Cant get enough of Adelaide? We can't fault you for wanting to stay longer. Don't worry, there's still plenty to do if you're thinking about extending your stay. Add these recommendations to your list and stay in vacay mode a little bit longer.
