Hentley Farm Restaurant wins one MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

At Hentley Farm, lunch starts outside the window.

Through it, you can see Greenock Creek snaking between hollow-limbed river red gums, their pale bark mottled with grey and pink. Beyond the water, rows of shiraz climb the red contours of the western ridge, bare in the winter chill or heavy with purple clusters in late summer. Perched among it all is a humble dining room, Hentley Farm, home to One MICHELIN Star. Just off the kitchen door, timber garden beds hum with bees and fat leaves of cavolo nero stretch toward the light. What the team can’t coax from the soil or forage along the creek flats, they get from nearby Barossa farmers, orchardists and backyard growers. From there, many hands take over. Executive Chef Kyle Johns shapes the menu around the season, while chief winemaker Andrew Quin works alongside him for a different taste of the same farm. Front-of-house staff read the table, pour with a storyteller’s ease and place each course down with a smile. 

“We come to work and just do what we love doing,” Kyle says. “And getting an accolade for that is a massive achievement, especially for me and my team.” The star belongs to the work of all of them.

Visit Hentley Farm Restaurant

The farm comes first

Marigolds flare gold along the creek flats before finding their way inside, scattered across plates or standing in little vases on the table. Vegetables push up through Hentley Farm’s garden beds, while growers from across the Barossa bring in boxes of whatever is sweetest, ripest or simply too good to leave behind that week. “I go directly to the source, and the person who’s delivering it to me is the person who’s raised it and grown it,” Kyle says. Kyle wants ingredients to still feel like themselves by the time they reach the table. He’ll take something familiar, draw out its sweetness or depth, then leave enough of it untouched that you still recognise what was pulled from the ground in the first place. He pays just as much attention to the parts that might otherwise be left behind. Waste is considered before a dish even makes the menu. If it creates too much, it may not make the cut. Scraps go to the chickens or the compost pile, which, in time, works its way back into the garden beds. Through the cooler months, sheep graze between the vines, keeping the rows in check. Chef Kyle works closely with chief winemaker Andrew Quin, building dishes that can stand beside wines grown on the estate rather than treating the pairing as an afterthought. Sometimes the plate brings something new out of the glass. Sometimes the glass sends you back for another forkful.  For Kyle, it’s another way of cooking with what’s around him, rather than against it.

I go directly to the source, and the person who’s delivering it to me is the person who’s raised it and grown it. - Kyle Johns

The Chef

Kyle took the scenic route to Hentley Farm. Born in South Africa, he began his career in Sydney, clocking his fair share of hours in heavy-hitting kitchens like Otto and Flying Fish. Then he and his wife packed a van and headed around Australia. Along the way came a cattle station, a burger truck at a Western Australian rodeo and a short stint at Appellation in the Barossa. He was immediately drawn to the region, because eating and drinking is a way of life here. But, they chose to keep driving.Until the Barossa called him back. Returning to the region, Kyle remembers thinking, “Well, this is a life". Hentley Farm gave him a place to make something of it, and a community just as willing to share what it grows. Between the vineyard, the garden and the people growing food around him, Kyle is never short of somewhere to start.

 Day 1

Hentley Farm and a MICHELIN-Key stay

Start at Hentley Farm’s cellar door in its 1840s cottage, then wander across to the restaurant and clear the afternoon. The Discovery menu stretches over lunch, with Greenock Creek below the gums and shiraz vines just beyond the glass. When the last course is cleared, check into Le Mas Barossa in Rowland Flat. The tiny four-room hotel has just been awarded one MICHELIN Key, so the spoiling needn’t stop when lunch does.

Your One-MICHELIN-Star lunch

Hentley Farm Restaurant opens for lunch only, Thursday to Sunday, with a few ways to settle in. Take the full 'discovery' menu across the afternoon, choose the shorter 'petite discovery' in The Barn, or gather six to eight around the Chef’s Table beside the open kitchen.

Top dishes to order

The menu here changes with the season, sometimes week to week, so there’s no single dish to chase. The top order is the full discovery menu. Through floor-to-ceiling glass, Greenock Creek winds past the flats where the kitchen forages and the beauty block’s shiraz rolls toward the ridge. Add the luxury wine pairing and taste that view, plate by plate and glass by glass.

Day 2

See Barossa from above

Set the alarm early (very early) for a sunrise balloon flight over the Barossa. Barossa Balloon Adventures meet guests ahead of sunrise, so you can float over vineyards and villages while watching the first light of day. 
Once your feet are firmly back on the ground, take the palm-lined road to Seppeltsfield Wines and step into the Centennial Cellar, where a barrel of tawny has been set aside from every vintage since 1878. Next door, JamFactory at Seppeltsfield fills the old stables with ceramics, glass, jewellery and leather, with working studios where you can watch makers at the bench. Then finish somewhere easy, perhaps Maggie Beer's Farm Shop, collecting a few Barossa staples for the drive home.
