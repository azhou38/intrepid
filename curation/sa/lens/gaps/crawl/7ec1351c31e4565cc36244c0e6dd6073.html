Welcome to Adelaide, South Australia

The Lane Vineyard, Adelaide Hills

Discover the best of South Australia

Travel to an island full of iconic Australian wildlife or to the outback where untamed landscapes are just waiting to be discovered. The word has now landed around the world; Lonely Planet has named the Flinders Ranges & Outback as one of the world's top travel destinations for 2026. Dine at award-winning restaurants recognised by the MICHELIN Guide and sip on award-winning wines.

Whether you desire an off-road wild adventure in region or fly in luxury to the boutique city of Adelaide. South Australia offers the best of Australia all in one holiday. 

 

A Guide to Discover South Australia

Make your South Australian holiday memorable. Here are the top things you must see and do in South Australia. Dare to swim with a Great White Shark? Or dine in a 120 year old fig tree? Go off-grid and 4WD on tracks of red-glistening dirt or snorkel off the pristine coastlines of blue shores. Time to tick it off your bucket list and plan your trip to South Australia.

Meet the restaurants behind the Stars

A MICHELIN Star is only the start. Get to know the chefs, growers and makers behind South Australia’s most celebrated tables, and the stories that put each dish on the plate. Then make a day of it, with local experiences and itineraries that keep the long lunch going well.

  • Magill Estate Restaurant

    Magill Estate Restaurant cooks around the cellar

    Scott Huggins
    Magill Estate Restaurant (MER)

    MICHELIN Stars, South Australia 2027

    Experience 180 years of winemaking and an Executive Chef who won’t take the credit alone. This is Magill Estate Restaurant, where local produce and old-vintage wines shape every plate.
  • Arkhé

    Everything at Arkhé meets open flame before it meets you

    Jake Kellie
    Arkhé

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    Everything here meets fire first. Jake Kellie’s wood oven turns local wagyu and lobster into the food that earned Arkhé one of Australia’s first MICHELIN-Stars.

     

  • Restaurant Aptos

    Restaurant Aptos brings native Australia to the table

    Justin James
    Restaurant Aptos

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    Inside an 1800s church in the Adelaide Hills, 14 guests at a time eat their way across 16 courses of native Australia. Come curious and expect to get involved.

     

  • Restaurant Botanic

    Eat what the garden gives up at Restaurant Botanic

    Jamie Musgraves
    Restaurant Botanic

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    The Adelaide Botanic Garden frames the view, then it lands on your plate. Executive Chef Jamie Musgraves’ 15-course menu changes with every season, so no two visits are quite the same.

     

  • Esmay

    Esmay is a love letter to South Australian producers

    Alanna Sapwell
    Esmay

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    Nostalgia on a fork, served inside a 140-year-old former pub. Chef Alanna Sapwell-Stone’s love letter to South Australian growers is part grandmother’s kitchen, part world-class pass.

  • LVN Restaurant

    Dinner at LVN Restaurant is like dining in a painting

    Jacob Davey
    LVN

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    At the end of a winding private drive, a glasshouse restaurant treats the Adelaide Hills like a canvas. The menu starts in the garden and is built backwards from the wine.

  • Hentley Farm Restaurant

    Lunch starts outside the window at Hentley Farm Restaurant

    Kyle Johns
    Hentley Farm Restaurant

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    Lunch starts outside the window. Chef Kyle Johns builds the menu from the garden, the creek flats and growers down the road, with the winemaker working alongside.

     

  • Maxwell Restaurant

    At Maxwell Restaurant the vineyard sets the menu

    Matt Rogers and Fabian Lehmann
    Maxwell Restaurant

    MICHELIN Star, South Australia 2027

    Tucked into a limestone hill in McLaren Vale, this family-owned restaurant builds a multi-course tasting menu around South Australian produce and the family’s own wines.

South Australian wine time

British wine expert and TV presenter Helen McGinn has long sung the praises of Australian wine, and South Australia’s bottles are never far from the top of her list. This time, she’s bringing great friend and fellow taste-maker Kat Farmer straight to the source. To the vineyards. To the cellar doors. To the people who’ve spent years among these vines and still get excited to share stories about this Great Wine Capital of the world.

The pair travel from Adelaide along the Epicurean Way, winding through some of the country’s most celebrated wine regions, staying in boutique lodges. You can too. Imagine sun-warmed afternoons at a table set between the vines, where wine and the produce on your plate share the same postcode.

The itinerary writes itself. Blend your own bottle, laughing as you debate the perfect percentage. E-bike between cellar doors along gum-lined roads or end the afternoon with sandy feet after a quick dip in the ocean. Over eight delicious days, you can follow in Helen and Kat’s footsteps and enjoy your own South Australian holiday. You can meet the makers, growers and locals who value living well over living large. Hear the stories of ancient vines and even older landscapes. And somewhere between the first sip and the last course, you’ll realise it’s not just about the wine, it’s about the simple moments that make it all taste better. Fly with Singapore Airlines to Adelaide and use this itinerary as your starting point.
