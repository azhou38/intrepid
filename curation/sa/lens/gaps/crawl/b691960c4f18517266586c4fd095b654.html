Restaurant Botanic wins One MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

Tucked inside Adelaide Botanic Garden, this sanctuary turns wild native ingredients into an extraordinary 15-course performance. 

The drop in temperature hits you first. It happens almost as soon as you step into the Adelaide Botanic Garden, where ferns, shrubs and 100-year-old Moreton Bay fig trees hug you in. A few steps deeper and nature closes in on you, shutting out the noise of traffic and the sight of concrete. The city garden is a sanctuary. Restaurant Botanic is its temple. Housed within a historic rotunda, the restaurant is bathed in tree-dappled light. Inside, the space is draped in soft eucalypt and earthy hues, blurring the line between the garden beyond and the kitchen within. Seated in the restaurant, the near-panoramic glass windows frame the 50 hectares of inner-city greenery that is about to be tasted. The garden provides bountiful botanicals, and in return, the chefs honour this ancient bounty by giving every “ingredient the platform it deserves”. Geraldton wax, scented emu bush, lemon aspen leaf, paperbark and tea tree. Ingredients that you didn’t even know you could eat. They all become the hero of their own dish. “We focus on Australian ingredients, small purveyors and suppliers, and spotlighting the best produce we can get,” says Restaurant Botanic Executive Chef Jamie Musgraves. 

Visit Restaurant Botanic

Foraged and found

Have you ever eaten a tart shell made of kangaroo grass and calamari, filled with a wattle seed miso cream and topped with Hamachi that’s been cured with lemon-scented eucalyptus and seasoned with sunrise lime and bloodroot? It’s unlikely, unless you have dined here. Something so unforgivingly Australian, with a twist of artistry that has the menu reading like notes from a botanical experiment, is the signature mark of a Restaurant Botanic dish. And these dishes have a fleeting lifespan. The team here craft a new 15-course tasting menu with the turn of each season, meaning no two visits are likely the same. Everything is informed by the garden that envelop this space – what it grows and what it gives. In winter, you might find bull kelp on the menu. Come spring, the menu is raked like dead foliage, clearing space for what’s newly in bloom - like a green macadamia emulsion. The 20 seats at Restaurant Botanic are shaped around their view. Pull up a chair at the chef’s table and be treated to a night at the theatre, as chefs weave, chop and plate in a carefully choreographed rhythm. A seat in the main restaurant will see you watching a different kind of show, as the last light of day falls between the trees, pooling in the foliage. 

We lean into a lot of the seafood that is here, like Kangaroo Island abalone, also some of the snapper that is caught off Robe and some of the meats here...[like] kangaroo and venison. - Jamie Musgraves

The Chef

From humble beginnings in Western Australia to the bustling streets of Hong Kong, Jamie Musgraves resume is as varied as his degustation menu. Having refined his skills at institutions like Vasse Felix and Rockpool Bar and Grill, Jamie went on to something more experimental – cooking for the nomadic pop-up restaurant concept ‘One Star House Party’ in Hong Kong. In 2021, he made the move to South Australia to join the team at Restaurant Botanic, before becoming Executive Chef in 2024 after Justin James’s (of Restaurant Aptos) departure. Jamie describes cooking with native ingredients as “instinctual,” after growing up in the surf, snorkeling through golden kelp and drying off next to the saltbush. These ingredients have surrounded him his whole life, but it was time on Country learning about First Nations culture and foraging practices that opened his eyes to their versatility.

 Day 1: The Native Botanical Immersion

The Native Botanical Immersion 

Start your day where the night ends – with a walk through the Adelaide Botanic Garden. Explore this sanctuary in a different light and spend time noticing the hundreds of diverse plant species. Chances are, you’ll be dining on some tonight. Next, make the short walk to the National Wine Centre of Australia. Showcasing all 65 Australian wine regions, it’s the perfect way to prepare your palate for a celebration of all things Australian grown and foraged. Book an interactive tasting experience or get behind the scenes access to the largest open cellar in the Southern Hemisphere when you upgrade to a cellar tasting. 

Where to stay 

Book in at EOS by SkyCity and you’ll be steps from North Terrace and easy walking distance to the Garden. 

Your One-MICHELIN-Star dinner

Top dish

No two menus look the same, so expect the unexpected. Settle in for a multi-hour degustation that will see your taste buds treated to everything from inventive ferments to rare flora and fauna. One thing is for sure; you’re likely to try something you’ve never tasted before.

Day 2: Market Producers and Laneway Cocktail Bars

Market Producers and Laneway Cocktail Bars 

Duck across the CBD and start your morning at the Adelaide Central Market. Recently named one of the top three food markets globally, we suggest joining a guided tasting tour so you don’t miss any local favourites. Meet multi-generational stallholders and hear how local produce has shaped their family life. Next, meander down the gastronomical lanes of Leigh and Peel Street – home to some of Adelaide’s best bars and restaurants. Book in for lunch at a local favourite like Shobosho, Gondola Gondola or Fugazzi (for something heartier). Let your food settle as you stroll back to North Terrace and spend the late afternoon stopping between the Art Gallery of South Australia, the South Australian Museum and MOD. 

 

Room for more?

Keep the culinary journey going...

Continue your MICHELIN journey with a night at Adelaide’s flame-powered Arkhé. Order the Mayura Station tomahawk, dry-aged 30 days, crusted in smoked chocolate and finished over open flame.
