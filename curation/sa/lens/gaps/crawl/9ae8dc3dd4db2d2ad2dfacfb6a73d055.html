Esmay wins One MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

Jersey curd with peaches and rose sounds like something your grandmother might have made, if your grandmother had run a world-class pass.


Instead, it comes from Executive Chef Alanna Sapwell of Esmay, serving up food that tastes like nostalgia on a fork. Housed inside what was a 140-year-old Adelaide pub, Esmay is far from parmi-and-a-pint territory. It’s a love letter to South Australian growers, and this year, it earned Esmay One MICHELIN Star.

Alanna describes it as 'Country Women's Association food, but elevated', made by someone who trained in some of the country's most demanding kitchens. Strip away the fine dining reputation, and what's left is simple: produce that speaks for itself.

New creations turn up on the menu almost weekly, with each dish deliberately placed next to something familiar, so the unfamiliar never feels like a leap. Dig into a tomato and red pepper terrine, topped with mud crab and dressed in wild celery seed vinegar, or venison served with a bone marrow sauce their regulars call “naughty”.  

That nostalgia isn’t confined to the kitchen. It spills out into the meticulously styled 40-seater space, where silver platters catch the light and duck-shaped decanters look like they've been pulled straight from your nanna's cabinet. Even the kitchen tools are tended to with the kind of pride you'd expect to find in your grandad's shed. It makes for a room as steeped in memory as the menu.

Visit Esmay

Produce, produce, produce

Esmay was never supposed to put down roots. It started as a pop-up in 2020, Alanna taking it on the road from there to the Ritz-Carlton in Perth, heritage pubs in Brisbane and wine bars in Canberra. Then a local hospitality group behind the likes of Restaurant Botanic stepped in, backing Alanna to finally give it a permanent home. A drive into the Adelaide Hills was all it took to convince her to stay.

South Australia's regional food bowls offered something her moving restaurant could never tap into: deep, enduring relationships with the people growing her ingredients. That proximity shows up everywhere on the plate, from local coastal fishers supplying line-caught King George whiting, to Fleurieu Peninsula producers providing fan fillet for her emu pastrami donuts. She also forages for whatever the gardens feel like giving up that season, like wild celery seed that goes into the vinegar for her tomato and red pepper terrine. For a chef who spent years on the move, staying still has given her the rarest ingredient of all: a sense of place. 

I think that’s what hospitality is, it’s community - Alanna Sapwell

The chef

Alanna has sharpened her craft across a globe-trotting career spanning destinations like Noosa, Italy, Japan and Arnhem Land. During that time, she ran the pass at Sydney’s three-hatted Saint Peter and earned a Chef Hat of her own at Brisbane’s ARC Dining, cementing an uncompromising commitment to ethical, sustainable farming. Settling in Adelaide, she found an entire state that shared her obsession with produce, and a kitchen where her decades of dedication paid off in full. "After 26 years in the industry, I get to tick [MICHELIN] off my list," she says, "which I never thought was possible."

Esmay Itinerary

Where to stay

Crowne Plaza Adelaide, right in the East End, surrounded by boutique shops, bars and restaurants. It's also next door to Honeydripper, which is perfect for a pre-dinner drink before the short walk (20 minutes) to Esmay.

Wander the Adelaide Botanic Gardens

Spend the morning roaming the Adelaide Botanic Garden and join the Aboriginal Native Plant and Social History Tour to learn how native plants have fed and sustained people here for generations, then grab lunch at Botanic Lodge, part of the same hospitality group as Esmay. From there, follow the Torrens riverbank back into town.

Your One-MICHELIN-Star dinner

Top dish

Tonight, all roads lead to Esmay. It’s hard to suggest a single dish when the menu changes weekly. Just know, whatever's on the menu when you visit will taste like a nostalgic bite of Australia.

Follow the road that changed everything

Drive the same route into the Adelaide Hills that convinced Esmay's chef to put down roots for good. Stop at a cellar door or two, like Deviation Road or Shaw + Smith, and keep an eye out for roadside stalls selling fresh flowers, honey and whatever else the season's producing along the way.

Continue the drive up to Hahndorf, which is Australia's oldest surviving German settlement, and home to Beerenberg Farm. In strawberry season (November to April), spend an hour wandering the patch and picking your own fresh produce to take home. Hungry? Book a lunch at Thelma, a bistro built entirely around local, seasonal produce from just down the road. Looking for dinner closer to home? Golden Boy's Tuk Tuk sharing menu is an East End favourite close to the Crowne Plaza. Alternatively, stay in with a bite at Koomo for Japanese with a view of Adelaide’s skyline (it’s on top of where you're sleeping).
