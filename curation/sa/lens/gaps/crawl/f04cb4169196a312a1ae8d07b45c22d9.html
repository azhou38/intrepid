Restaurant Aptos wins One MICHELIN Star

14 OCT 2026

In an 1800s sandstone church tucked into the damp green folds of Stirling, 14 diners at a time are eating their way through a very different idea of Australia.

Your first bite could come from a branch. Marron might arrive in its shell with a claw to help you eat it. Later, you could find yourself drinking from a hollowed emu egg. Restaurant Aptos is fine dining cut loose from convention, and now, officially recognised with One MICHELIN Star.

Chef-owner Justin James knew he’d found the home for Aptos the first time he pulled up outside the former church in Stirling, around two years before opening. “The church chose me,” he says. “I instantly fell in love with it and knew this was the place.”

Now, dinner has become a pilgrimage. Guests move across three dining rooms, carried along by six playlists that change in tempo and personality with the courses. Instead of sitting back and watching each plate arrive, you’re asked to get involved. Pick things up. Turn them over. Work out how to eat them. Occasionally, you’ll need instructions.

“We always say we don’t take ourselves too seriously, but we’re serious about what we do,” Justin says.

That’s the key to Aptos. Under all the fun and theatrics is a kitchen that sweats every detail. A single bite might take hours to get right, and you’ll have it gone in seconds, fingers and all. The precision is theirs, the pleasure is all yours.

Visit Aptos

Eating Australia

Start outside in the crisp Hills air. Around 600 native plants have been bedded into the church grounds. Mountain pepper rubs shoulders with native thyme, alongside wild pockets of muntries, quandongs, river mint and warrigal greens. Some will eventually make the short trip through the church doors and into the kitchen.

When Justin began developing Aptos, he set himself a rule: every dish needed to contain at least one native Australian ingredient, usually more, and that ingredient had to earn its place. A token pinch of dried saltbush to tick a provenance box wouldn’t do. 

“Restaurant Aptos is all about Australia through the team’s eyes,” he says. “We source ingredients only found in Australia, from north to south, east to west, and use them across 16 courses to tell a story and take guests on a journey.”

Recent menus show where that can lead. Green ants bring a sharp little kick to abalone. Lemon myrtle threads through warm sauce spooned over marron. Ingredients you may have walked past in the bush without noticing suddenly have your full attention. Even the things carrying the food keep pointing you back outside. Bunya nuts and gumnuts become vessels. Some courses arrive in shells or on objects that look as though they have been gathered rather than ordered from a catering catalogue. Beside each course sits a small postcard explaining what you’re tasting and, when needed, how to tackle it. You don’t need to know your muntries from your quandongs when you walk in, but you may well when you walk out.

We always say we don't take ourselves too seriously, but we're serious about what we do. - Justin James

The chef

Justin grew up on a farm outside Detroit, where deep freezes and summer thaws dictated what grew, what simmered on the stove and what you did outside. He set his sights on the kitchen early, deciding he wanted to become a chef while he was still at school. The next two decades took him a long way from that Michigan farm and into some formidable kitchens – including New York’s Three-MICHELIN-Star restaurant Eleven Madison Park.

There were leading roles at Vue de Monde in Melbourne and, later, Restaurant Botanic in Adelaide, where native Australian ingredients moved closer and closer to the centre of his cooking. Justin has described his venture at Aptos as the culmination of more than 20 years in kitchens, but for all the high-wire precision behind it, his reason for cooking is simple.

“I love food, but that’s probably not the main reason I’m a chef,” he says. “I like to make people feel special. I want to see people happy.”

The One MICHELIN Star tells you Aptos is worth the journey. As for what you’ll be served, you’ll have to book a table to find out.

A day in the Hills

Take the long way to dinner.

“I mean, the Adelaide Hills is one of the most beautiful places in the world,” Justin says. “It's almost like a mini vacation every day.”

It's not hard to see his point. The drive up from Adelaide takes you past towering gums and winding roads, with cellar doors and little country towns tempting you to pull over along the way. Dinner may be the booking that brought you here, but give the day some room around it.

Ease into the Hills with a chauffeured food and wine tour, stopping for cool-climate pours and produce grown close by. Just don't overdo lunch… Aptos is waiting. By early evening, make your way to the old church in Stirling for a drink at Bar Cruz or a dram at tiny Bar Mary before dinner. Then, hand your night over to Justin James and his team.

Where to stay

Stay up in the Hills at Sequoia Lodge, an adults-only retreat overlooking Piccadilly Valley. There are just 14 suites - fittingly the same number of guests Aptos seats at a time - along with deep baths, fireplaces and spring-fed hot pools waiting when dinner is done. 

Your One-MICHELIN-Star dinner

Top dish

Aptos serves a 16-course tasting menu, with some details deliberately kept back until you’ve taken the first bite. Ask Justin which dish people will still be talking about on the drive home and he won’t tell you what’s in it. “It’s kind of a secret,” he says. Probably a good cue to stop Googling. 

Start the morning at Mount Lofty Botanic Garden, where paths dip into ferny gullies and climb beneath branches that change with the season. In spring, magnolias crowd the canopy. By autumn, the colour has dropped to your feet. 

From there, head to Udder Delights in Verdun for a taste of Adelaide Hills cheese or get your hands into a cheesemaking class when dates align. 

For lunch, wind along the back roads to LVN at Bird in Hand. Settle into the sun-warmed dining room overlooking rows of vines for courses pulled straight from the estate garden, matched with cold-climate wines poured direct from the cellar.

Book your table

Come hungry, leave convinced. Pull up a seat and taste why this haunt won its spot in Australia's first MICHELIN Guide.
