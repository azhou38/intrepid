You judge photos for ONE group by LOOKING at them and reading the file page of the one you choose (description, categories, coordinates, licence). Start from the contact sheets the script drew: `sheets/<slot>.jpg` for each `search` slot (numbered as in `candidates.json`, with width, licence, distance from the pin and source under each) and `sheets/confirm.jpg` for the current photos of the `confirm` slots, one Read each. Then open only your pick at 960 px (for the four checks and `hero-crop.py`) and its file page; never fetch every candidate's page. A candidate whose thumbnail could not be loaded is listed under `_unresolved`: say so; it is not a reject. A file name, search rank or matching title proves nothing. Rules: `IMAGES.md` → "The four checks" plus the slot type's section; the checklist below is those rules in brief, so open the guide only for a case it doesn't settle.

The four checks (one failure rejects; if you can't tell it's a photograph, it fails)
1. Right place — for any shared or vague name (Clyde, Blue Lake, Nelson Market), proven on the file page; reject files about people, films, ships, other countries. (Visitors or animals in the frame are fine when the place, not they, is the focus of the photo.)
2. Shows the named thing itself — the lake, not the township beside it; the view, if visitors come for the view (a lookout's shelter is not its view).
3. Headers and why-visit: spectacular and wide (a town in its landscape, never a house or street corner). Spots: clear and recognisable at thumbnail size.
4. A real current colour photograph — no drawing, map, satellite/aerial (headers), render, collage, archive or B&W scan.
Also: too dark is not a rejection (IMAGES.md → "Brightness": the reconciler brightens anything below the floor), but note `dark: true` on a photo whose subject is hard to make out, unless it is dark on purpose (night, cave, sunset, aurora). Header ≥ 2000 px and aspect 0.6–2.0; why-visit ≥ 1280 px and must show its reason; spot ≥ 960 px; no repeats. Licence: CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA and CC BY-ND pass; the app's crop, zoom and brightening count as display, so an ND photo may take a `hero_crop`. Any NC licence fails. An official photo passes: "used with permission" from a tourism board or parks agency, and "permission not confirmed" (still flagged) from any other owner. A sheet holds Commons photos and Google Images hits together, ranked by source: between two photos that pass equally well, choose the one higher on the sheet (an open licence over "official photo, used with permission" over "web photo, permission not confirmed"), and open a Google hit's `page` to check it is the place.
Crop (every photo; IMAGES.md → "Keeping the subject in the crop"): every crop is near-square (heroes, headers, cards, pins) or 1.6:1 (why-visit cards), so a 16:9 photo shows only the middle 53% of its width in a square. Run `python3 scripts/curation/hero-crop.py <thumbnail> [--x N --y N --zoom Z]` and LOOK at the crop: the named object must be inside it, recognisable and near the middle (not cut by an edge, not a sliver at one side, not lost in sky or ground). Off-centre but fixable → record `hero_crop: {x, y}`, where the subject sits in the photo (0 = left/top edge, 1 = right/bottom), after checking it with --x/--y; a tall subject usually needs y near its top. A landscape photo (aspect > 1.1) shows its FULL height in the square, so `y` alone cannot move anything: if more than about half the square is sky, ground or empty water around a small or low/high subject (a bay under a big sky, a summit at the top edge), record `hero_crop: {x, y, zoom}` (zoom ~1.4–2) and check it with `--zoom`; never call such a crop `ok`. Unfixable (a sliver of a wide panorama, wider than the crop, or a subject that stays tiny or soft even zoomed) → reject, with `hero_crop: "fail: <why>"`. Do this for every photo you accept or keep-current whose aspect is outside 0.8–1.25 (1.3–2.0 for why-visit), or whose object is not clearly central.

First judge (`judge-1.json`): for every `confirm` slot in `task.json`, judge the current photo only → `keep-current`, or `reject` with the failed check (that slot then goes to search). For every `search` slot that has candidates, judge them from its contact sheet → a `choice` or `"none: <why>"`. A slot with no candidates is not yours: it goes to the searcher.
Second judge (blind — never read any judge-1 file): you get one batch of about 35 slots from any groups (`second/batch-NN.json`, from `scripts/curation/second-judge.py build`), only those where a first judge proposed a new file; for each, pick a `choice` independently from its candidates and the current photo, and write `second/batch-NN.out.json` keyed `<group>|<slot>`. The script has already checked size, aspect, licence, a file used by another spot, brightness and crop share, and dropped the failures: judge only what needs eyes (right place, the thing itself, a real photograph, the subject inside the crop shown beside each photo on the slot's sheet). Keep-current decisions don't come to you.
Format per slot: `{ candidates: [{ file, right_place, shows_named_thing, spectacular_wide, real_photograph, verdict, reason }], choice }`.
Benchmark you must reproduce: Clyde, Hamilton, Blue Lake, the 1913 Government Gardens and the archive Blue Lake photo are all rejected.

Sharing within a destination is allowed and often best (IMAGES.md → "Why visit photos"): a file may be a destination's header, one of its why-visit photos and the photo of its spots at once. When you judge a slot, also say if a photo already used for another slot of the same destination (its header, a why-visit photo or one of its spots) beats the current one here, and recommend it; reuse across different destinations still fails.

Proof and audit: `task.json` slots may carry `proof` lines (what `photo-prove.py` established) and the flag "audit sample: the script proved this one; judge it blind". Judge an audit-sample slot like any other, from the image and file page, ignoring the proof; a reject there tells the owner the script's proof is too lenient. Slots the script proved and did not sample never reach you.
Unique subject (spots only; IMAGES.md → "Spot photos"): before accepting a spot photo, look at the photos of its neighbours (spots within about 10 km whose name or bio mentions it, or that it mentions: a park and the waterfall inside it, a town and its bridge) and reject a photo that shows the same subject as one of them, or the neighbour's own file. Say which neighbour it duplicates. The spot that IS the subject keeps the photo; the broader place needs a photo of something else, or `none`.
- A stand-in is a reject: the photo must show the named place itself (not a nearby view, a similar species, another venue, or a photo whose focus is a generic animal or person with the place barely visible). People and animals are fine when they are not the main focus: visitors in a pool or on a boardwalk, an animal in its enclosure with the park around it. If only stand-ins exist the answer is `none`, and the spot becomes NO_PHOTO (IMAGES.md, "A photo shows the place itself").
