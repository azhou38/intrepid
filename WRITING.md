# Writing: names, fields and copy for destinations and spots

How to write each entry in `src/data/destinations.ts` and `src/data/spots.ts` so it reads well and behaves correctly in the app: names, every field, the voice for descriptions, reasons, taglines and bios, and the checklist to run before finishing. Which places belong, and where each goes, is in `COVERAGE.md`; photos are in `IMAGES.md`.

## Names (countries, destinations and spots)

Applies to every `name` shown in the app.

- **Just the place's own name.** Never add the destination, region or country to a spot's name: "Russell", not "Russell, New Zealand"; "Queens Park", not "Queens Park, Invercargill"; "Mount Victoria", not "Mount Victoria, Wellington". The app already shows where a place is. The same goes for a destination's name ("Wellington", not "Wellington, New Zealand").
- **Capitalise each word.** Title case: every word starts with a capital ("Te Paki Sand Dunes", "Hundertwasser Toilets", "Wellington Waterfront"). Short joining words (*of, the, and, to, in, on, a, de, la*…) stay lowercase unless they open the name ("Craters of the Moon", "Bridge to Nowhere", "Bay of Islands"). Country names follow the same rule ("Côte d'Ivoire", "United States").
- **A needed disambiguator belongs in `wiki`, not `name`.** If the Wikipedia article is titled "Russell, New Zealand" or "Blue Lake (Rotorua)", put that exact title in the spot's `wiki` field (see "Writing a spot"); the shown name stays "Russell" or "Blue Lake". Two places may then share a shown name — that is fine, search results show which destination each belongs to.
- **Keep a local alternative name only when it is part of the name people use**, such as "Mykonos Town (Chora)". It is not a place qualifier.

## Writing a destination

Follow an existing entry in `src/data/destinations.ts` (they are the reference); every field below is required unless noted.

- `id` — lowercase, hyphenated, unique, stable (it is the key for saved data; never rename one once it ships; a destination replaced by its region gets a new id and its old one retires, so saved visits on the old destination are lost: say so). `name` — the common English name (the region travellers name when one covers the area: "Sapphire Coast", not a town in it; see `COVERAGE.md`), matching its Wikipedia article title where possible; it is also the key into `WIKI_IMAGE_OVERRIDES`.
- `country`, `countryCode` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2), `timezone` (IANA, e.g. `Pacific/Auckland`), `continent`.
- `coordinates` — the centre of the place as visitors understand it (a town's centre, a park's main visitor hub or visual centre), not a bureaucratic centroid.
- `category` — one of `city | park | beach | mountain | landmark | island | desert | ruin | nature | lake`. Drives the header-image rule (cities/towns need a cityscape; everything else a wide landscape) and the Explore sections, so pick what the place *is*, not what it is near.
- `defaultZoomSpanKm` — the span the map should fit when the destination opens: just big enough to contain the places visitors go within it (~15–30 km for a town, 40–70 km for a park or coast, 100+ km for a large region). **Every spot should lie within about 75% of this span of the destination's coordinates** — widen the span rather than leaving a headline spot off-screen.
- `icon` — a single emoji; `tagline` — one sentence that fills **exactly two lines** of the destination sheet's header: it must wrap onto a second line even on the widest phone and must not run to a third on the narrowest, where it would be cut off with "…" (roughly 60–85 characters; the limit is the measured width, so check with `python3 scripts/curation/tagline-fit.py --text "<tagline>"`, and run it with no arguments to check every destination; `apply-destination.py` refuses a tagline that fails); `description` — 3–4 sentences, concrete (what it is, why people go, one distinctive fact).
- `whyVisit` — **two to four** short reasons of **2–8 words each**, strongest first. **How many follows the destination's popularity** (its `rank`): **4** for the most popular (rank 1–2: Sydney, Queenstown, Paris), **3** for an average destination (rank 3–4), **2** for a less popular one (rank 5: Hay, a local base). Never pad a thin destination up to a number, and never cut a strong reason to reach one; a reason that has no honest photo is never filled with a weak one: a strong reason (one of the destination's real draws) keeps its wording with a grey card (an empty `whyVisitPhotos` slot, `IMAGES.md`), and a lesser one is reworded or dropped. Each reason: one plain action and its subject ("See takahē and hihi on Kāpiti Island", "Cross the Escarpment Track's swing bridges"), with no second clause, numbers or justification, and **never a bare name or noun phrase** ("The Len Lye Centre", "Tongariro Alpine Crossing", "Iconic skyline & culture" are not reasons: "See kinetic art at the Len Lye Centre", "Walk the Tongariro Alpine Crossing"; `lint-entries.ts` flags a reason that does not start with an action verb); detail belongs in the description and tips (the app opens its carousel on the first one, in the middle slot). `highlights` — one 2–3 word phrase per reason (as many as `whyVisit`). `whyVisitPhotos` — one Commons file name per reason, so as many as `whyVisit` (see `IMAGES.md`).
- `goodToKnow` — **two or three** practical tips that trip up first-timers (booking, weather, transport, etiquette, safety), strongest first. **How many follows the destination's popularity** (its `rank`): **3** for a popular destination (rank 1–3), **2** for a less popular one (rank 4–5). Never pad to a number. Each tip is an `icon`, a short `title` (2–5 words) and a `detail` of **one or two sentences that fit three to four lines on screen: 160 characters at most, aim for about 120**; the sheet shows the detail in full, so a long one becomes a wall of text. Say the one thing to do or avoid and why, with nothing else. Specific beats generic; no filler.
- `bestMonths` (1–12) and `bestTimeBlurb` containing exactly one `{months}` placeholder — from travel-guide consensus, written for the destination's own hemisphere (southern-hemisphere summer is Dec–Feb). The blurb is the When to Visit card's headline and fills **two or three lines** of it, with `{months}` replaced by the formatted months ("Dec–Feb", "Mar–May and Sep–Nov"): at least two lines on the widest phone, never a fourth on the narrowest (roughly 60–110 characters once the months are in; the limit is the measured width, so check with `python3 scripts/curation/blurb-fit.py --text "<blurb>" --months 12,1,2`, and run it with no arguments to check every destination; `apply-destination.py` refuses a blurb that fails). One sentence: when to go and the one reason why (the season's weather, a migration, a festival), nothing more.
- `seasonalTags` — only where a real recurring pattern drives crowds (a ski season, a festival, a migration); omit otherwise. Hemisphere-relative tags flip automatically south of the equator; explicit-month tags do not.

## Voice (descriptions, reasons, taglines and spot bios)

Applies to `tagline`, `description`, `whyVisit`, `highlights`, `goodToKnow` and every spot `bio`.

- **Facts from sources only.** Every claim traces to a source (Wikipedia, Wikivoyage, the operator or managing agency). Hours, prices and "best in the world" claims that cannot be sourced are cut, or flagged as approximate. Never invent.
- **Never name a source in the app's text.** No "Wikivoyage says", "according to Wikipedia", "per the brief", "(Lonely Planet)" or the like in a description, tagline, reason, tip, best-time text, bio or hours: state the fact plainly. The source goes in the notes and the pull request, not the copy. `lint-entries.ts` flags it as `source`.
- **Concrete, not marketing.** Say what the place is, why people go and one distinctive fact. No filler ("stunning", "must-see"), no superlatives that cannot be backed.
- **Promise only what is there whenever a visitor arrives.** The test `COVERAGE.md` applies to spots ("It is there whenever a visitor arrives") applies to every `whyVisit` reason, `highlight` and `tagline`, and to what a `description` invites visitors to see. A permanent sight or activity passes; a yearly pattern passes too (whale season, spring wildflowers, a migration), with its timing in `bestTimeBlurb` or a tip. A passing state never does: a volcano's eruption, plume or smoke, lava flowing, a one-off event, a temporary exhibition, a shipwreck that will break up. Rabaul shipped with "See Tavurvur smoke across Simpson Harbour" while the run's own notes said the last eruption was in 2014. History is fine as history in the description ("a 1994 eruption buried the town"); it is never a reason to visit. `lint-entries.ts` flags likely cases as `transient`.
- **No repetition** within an entry (description, reasons, tips and tagline each say something different) or across entries (two destinations should not share near-identical phrasing).
- **One sentence** for a `tagline` (exactly two lines of the header, about 60–85 characters: see "Writing a destination") and a spot `bio` (a spot `bio` is at least 10 words: say what it is, where, and the one thing that makes it worth a stop; `lint-entries.ts` flags a shorter one), and a spot `bio` fits three lines of the carousel card (about 120 characters; see "Writing a spot"); a `description` is 3–4 sentences.
- **Reasons match photos.** Choose the `whyVisit` reasons (two to four), then find a photo for each; if no honest photo exists, keep a strong reason with a grey card or rewrite a lesser one (see `IMAGES.md`).

Write in the app's voice: plain, specific, enthusiastic without superlatives you cannot back up. No marketing filler, no unverifiable "best in the world" claims, no repetition between description, reasons and tips.

## Writing a spot

- `id` — for a spot of a destination, `<destinationId>-<n>`, numbered from 1 in importance order and never reused. **A spot moved to another destination, or replaced in place, keeps its id** (saved visits are keyed on it) even though the prefix no longer matches (Whanganui Journey and Bridge to Nowhere are `whanganui-1`/`whanganui-2` under Whanganui National Park; Walter Peak High Country Farm replaced TSS Earnslaw as `queenstown-4`); new spots continue the destination's own numbering, taken from every id the file has ever held (git history), not just the ones left. For a standalone spot, its own lowercase hyphenated slug (`glenorchy`), unique among all spot and destination ids.
- `destinationId` — the parent destination's id, or **omitted** for a standalone spot, which then needs `country`, `countryCode`, `continent` and `timezone` (see "Standalone spots in the app" in `COVERAGE.md`).
- `name` — the name shown in the app, following "Names" above: the place's own name in title case, with no destination, country or other disambiguator.
- `wiki` — the **exact title of the spot's Wikipedia article**, only when it differs from `name` (usually a disambiguated title such as "Russell, New Zealand" or "Blue Lake (Rotorua)"). The app looks the spot's photo up by this title (`spotPhotoTitle`: `wiki`, else `name`), and `WIKI_IMAGE_OVERRIDES` and the image manifest are keyed by it, so a creative or abbreviated title silently loads the wrong photo or none. When you rename a spot, move its old name into `wiki` if that was its article title. Where the article title is itself ambiguous (a common lake or park name), give it a hand-picked photo in `WIKI_IMAGE_OVERRIDES`.
- `category` — `museum | landmark | monument | religious | nature | viewpoint | hike | entertainment | market | beach | historic`. `icon` — one emoji that suits it.
- `coordinates` — the spot's entrance or the point visitors stand at, accurate to ~100 m. **A trail, track, walk or hike (any linear route) is pinned roughly at the centre of the route, halfway along its length, not at the trailhead** (`scripts/curation/resolve-pin.py` returns the midpoint of the OpenStreetMap route); the trailhead goes in the bio or notes if it is useful. A summit, lookout or viewpoint is pinned on the feature itself. Never reuse the destination's centre for a spot that is somewhere else.
- `bio` — one sentence, **at least 10 words**, what it is and why it is worth a stop, and **no longer than three lines on the spot carousel card**, which cuts anything longer off with "…". That is roughly 120 characters, but the limit is the measured width, not a count: check with `python3 scripts/curation/bio-fit.py --text "<bio>"` (it wraps the bio in the card's own font at the narrowest supported phone, 375 pt), and run it with no arguments to check every spot. If a bio does not fit, cut words rather than facts that matter: drop the second clause, the place qualifier or the list's last item. `lint-entries.ts` flags a bio that is too long.
- `hours` — never a placeholder such as "Hours vary; check the operator": that is a field nobody researched (Sea Life Sydney shipped with it, and with no price or link). A real, current range (`9:00 AM – 5:00 PM`), `Open 24 hours` for open-access places, or a short qualifier (`Daytime tours`, `Tides permitting`) where fixed hours do not apply. `closedDays` (0 = Sunday) only for a regular weekly closure. `specialClosures` only for a fixed-date closure you are confident of.
- `visitHoursMin` / `visitHoursMax` — a range of hours a typical visitor spends, always a range. A quick stop starts at 0: a glance, drive-through or photo stop (a roadside icon, a statue, a tunnel) is `0`–`0.25` (shown as "0 – 15 min"), and a viewpoint or monument you stop at briefly is `0`–`0.5` ("0 – 30 min"). A range that tops out under an hour is shown in minutes at both ends ("0 – 15 min", "15 – 45 min"), so use quarter-hour steps (0.25, 0.5, 0.75); a range that reaches an hour is shown in hours at both ends ("0.5 – 1 hr", "1 – 1.5 hr"). One unit per range, never "30 min – 1 hr".
- **Cost** — **every spot has one or the other**: `free: true`, or a researched price. "Unknown" is not a value: a spot with neither is unfinished and is not added (lint-entries flags it). `free: true` for no charge; otherwise `costMin`/`costMax` in the local currency with `currency` as an ISO 4217 code (`NZD`, `EUR`, `GBP`, …; omit only for USD). Use a single price (`costMin === costMax`) or the real range (adult entry, standard tour). Approximate and rounded is fine; invented precision is not. Operator-run experiences (cruises, flights, bungy) list the typical adult price.
- `ticketUrl` — **look for it on every spot, and add it whenever the spot has one official site** (the operator or steward), whether or not it charges: attractions, museums, zoos, parks, tours, wineries, lifts and railways nearly always do, and a missing link on a paid attraction is a gap, not a choice. Omit it only for open squares, beaches, suburbs and districts with no one steward, and for attractions sold by many operators (a sound cruise, a scenic flight, a dive trip). For public-land trails and parks, the managing agency's page for that park (e.g. the Department of Conservation) is the official site. Never link a booking aggregator, a guess, or a page you have not verified:
  - Find the site from the operator's own name (or from a Wikipedia/Wikivoyage "official website" link), not by guessing a domain; a lookalike domain can belong to someone else.
  - Load the page and check its title or heading names the place. A plain HTTP 200 is not proof: some sites answer 200 for any path (a 404 page rendered by script), so a guessed deep link like `/ruakuri-cave` can look fine and be dead. Prefer the site's homepage, or a deep link you copied from that site's own navigation.
  - A site that blocks scripts (403, captcha, "Just a moment…", a Vercel checkpoint) must be checked in a real browser (the in-app browser): blocked is not a reason to leave the link out. Luna Park Sydney, Coronet Peak and The Remarkables all lost their links that way. Only if it still cannot be loaded in a browser, leave `ticketUrl` out and say so in the summary rather than guessing.
  - A guessed deep link that returns "Page not found" is not evidence the place has no site: search for its page (the agency's own search, or a web search restricted to its domain) before giving up.

## Photos

Every new destination needs a hand-picked header and three "Why visit" photos, resolved through the image manifest. All the rules, and how to run `scripts/photo-picker.ts`, are in `IMAGES.md`. Spot photos default to the spot's Wikipedia lead image, which is fine unless it is wrong for the spot.

## Before you finish

- `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` shows no new errors.
- `npx tsx scripts/check-destination-headers.ts` passes (or lists only destinations whose photos you are about to add).
- Ids unique across the file; every spot has a `destinationId` that exists or the four standalone fields; no two spots share a name.
- Each destination has enough surrounding spots to justify being a destination (see "The two levels" in `COVERAGE.md`); lone attractions are listed as standalone spots, not promoted.
- Every spot you added or moved has a pin from `python3 scripts/curation/resolve-pin.py` (HIGH or MEDIUM, with `coordinates_source` recorded); `lint-entries.ts` shows no `pin` line (no round coordinates) and no `pin-shared` line (no point reused from another spot) for it.
- `scripts/verify-coordinates.ts` run for the area when you have network access; coordinates look right on a map (a swapped sign puts a New Zealand spot in the Atlantic); every spot is within its destination's span (otherwise it is standalone, or the span is wrong).
- Every destination has two to four `whyVisit` (matching its popularity), the same number of `highlights` and `whyVisitPhotos`, and two or three `goodToKnow` (3 for rank 1–3, 2 for rank 4–5), each `detail` 160 characters or fewer; each `bestTimeBlurb` has one `{months}` and `python3 scripts/curation/blurb-fit.py --ids <new destination ids>` passes (two or three lines of the When to Visit card).
- `python3 scripts/curation/bio-fit.py` passes: no spot bio runs past the carousel card's three lines.
- `python3 scripts/curation/tagline-fit.py --ids <new destination ids>` passes: every new tagline fills exactly two lines of the header (two lines on the widest phone, no third on the narrowest).
- Costs have a `currency`, free spots say `free: true`, and every spot with one official site has a verified `ticketUrl` (see "Writing a spot"); no `ticketUrl` points anywhere you have not confirmed. Search the new spots for a paid attraction with no link before finishing.
- `npx tsx scripts/curation/lint-entries.ts --region <id> --offline` shows no `no-ticket` line for any spot you added or touched: none charges without a link, and none is left with no cost and not free.
- A destination's spots are in importance order, the headline attraction first: the map gives photo pins in that order where pins compete (see `COVERAGE.md` → "Pin priority on the map").
- Say plainly in your summary what you could not verify (hours, prices, links) — these are approximate until someone checks them.
